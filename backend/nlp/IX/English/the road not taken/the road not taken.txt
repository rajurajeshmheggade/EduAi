ROAD NOT TAKEN well-known poem about making choices, choices shape us. Robert Frost American poet who writes simply, but insightfully, about common, ordinary experiences. Two roads diverged yellow wood, sorry I could not travel both one traveller, long I stood looked down one far I could where bent undergrowth; Then took other, just fair, having perhaps better claim, Because was grassy wanted wear; Though passing there Had worn them really about same. both morning equally lay leaves no step had trodden black. Oh, I kept first another day! Yet knowing how way leads way, I doubted if I should ever come back. I shall telling sigh Somewhere ages ages hence; Two roads diverged wood, I — I took one less travelled by, has made all difference. ROBERT FROST
