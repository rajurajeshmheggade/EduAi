LEGEND NORTHLAND poem narrates legend old lady who angered Saint Peter because her greed. Away, away Northland, Where hours day few, nights so long winter they cannot sleep them through; Where they harness swift reindeer sledges, when snows; children look like bear’s cubs their funny, furry clothes: They tell them curious story — I don’t believe ’tis true; yet you may learn lesson If I tell tale you. Once, when good Saint Peter Lived world below, walked about it, preaching, Just he did, you know, He came door cottage, travelling round earth, Where little woman was making cakes, baking them hearth; being faint fasting, day was almost done, He asked her, her store cakes, give him single one. So she made very little cake, But baking lay, She looked it, thought seemed Too large give away. Therefore she kneaded another, still smaller one; But looked, when she turned over, large first had done. Then she took tiny scrap dough, rolled rolled flat; baked thin wafer — But she couldn’t part that. she said, “My cakes seem too small When I eat them myself yet too large give away.” So she put them shelf. Then good Saint Peter grew angry, he was hungry faint; surely such woman Was enough provoke saint. he said, “You far too selfish dwell human form, have both food shelter, fire keep you warm. Now, you shall build birds do, shall get your scanty food boring, boring, boring, All day hard, dry wood.” Then up she went through chimney, Never speaking word, out top flew woodpecker, she was changed bird. She had scarlet cap her head, was left same; But all rest her clothes were burned Black coal flame. every country schoolboy Has seen her wood, Where she lives trees till very day, Boring boring food. PHOEBE CARY
